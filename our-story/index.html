Our Story · A family house of handmade rugs

Professio is Latin for a declaration, a public promise. Ours is simple: every rug we place is handmade, honestly described, and worthy of being kept for generations.

Kayseri · Since 1703

Profession of My Ancestors

Professio is named for the work our family has handed down for six generations: from horse traders in Kayseri to a house of handmade silk.

  1. 1703

    Kayseri

    Our family has lived in Kayseri since at least the early eighteenth century: the oldest tax record bearing its name dates from 1703. For generations they traded horses and other riding and pack animals.

  2. c. 1877

    Hayriye Hanım's loom

    Carpets came into the family late in the nineteenth century, we believe around 1877, when our great-grandmother Hayriye Hanım began weaving to support her household. In those years the city's Jewish and Greek merchants bought the carpets woven by Turkish and Armenian women and sold them on to the wealthy markets of the day.

  3. Yarn for wages

    Mehmet Osman Efendi

    Her son, Mehmet Osman Efendi, was apprenticed to one of those merchants. Before long he asked to be paid in yarn instead of money and brought it home for his mother to weave. He sold her carpets, spent every coin on more yarn, and set up a second loom for her, then more. Women from neighbouring families and from among their relatives joined them, Hayriye Hanım taught them the craft, and soon four looms were finishing four carpets a month.

  4. The war years

    Wool, when silk ran out

    The wars of the early twentieth century were hard on the family, but the looms never stopped. Silk thread became almost impossible to find, so for some twenty years they wove in wool instead.

  5. Three sons

    Carpets, rugs and shoes

    Carpets were slow to make and costly to buy, so one branch of the family began weaving simpler, more affordable rugs that every kind of customer could reach, and the business grew across Türkiye. Mehmet Osman Efendi had three sons: one followed him into carpets, one built the new rug trade, and one went into shoemaking.

  6. 1990s – 2000s

    The machine age

    Guided by its elders, the family wove carpets and rugs until the 1990s, when machine-made carpets arrived and prices across the home market fell sharply. In the early 2000s it stopped making rugs altogether, and economic turmoil brought carpet production at home down to a small scale.

  7. Today

    Six generations

    As the trade changed, the family began weaving in other countries and selling where the pieces were made. The methods have changed a little; the materials have not. Our Persian carpets are still woven from pure silk spun by real silkworms and dyed with real madder. Six generations on, it is still our profession.

The Roots

Raised among the stacks

Our earliest memories smell of wool and dust and cedar. Rolled rugs taller than we were. Afternoons spent learning to turn a corner back and count the knots.

The business has always belonged to the family. We buy from the weavers and workshops we know by name, we keep pieces in our own archive, and the person who answers your enquiry is the same person who unrolled the rug and checked every knot of it.

That closeness is our whole method. It is why we can tell you which piece is pure silk and which is not, which is new and which has lived a life, and why we put our name to a certificate for every one.

Our Declaration

The Certificate of Authenticity

Each rug leaves us with a certificate, signed by the family and issued in the name of its new custodian, affirming that it is handmade.

It records our promise and travels with the piece: to your heirs, to an insurer, to a future collector. If you are ever unsure about a Professio rug, write to us and we will confirm it from our records.
